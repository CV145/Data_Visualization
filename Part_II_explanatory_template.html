by Carlos Valeriano¶

Investigation Overview¶

My goal is to analyze the factors influencing loan interest rates (BorrowerAPR) in the Prosper dataset.

Dataset Overview and Executive Summary¶

This dataset is the Prosper Loan Data dataset. It contains 113,937 loans with 81 variables on each loan, including loan amount, borrower rate (or interest rate), current loan status, borrower income, and many others. The listings span from late 2005 to early 2014.

Key Insights:

  • The APR distribution centered around 15-20% with a notable spike at 35% representing the platform's maximum interest rate cap.
  • Loan amounts cluster at increments of $5,000 with lower loan amounts being the most frequently requested.
  • As borrower risk rating worsens from AA to HR, the median BorrowerAPR increases as shown in the box plot.
  • In bivariate analysis, the loans appeared to correlate with lower APRs. However the multivariate facet grid showed that within any credit tier the relationship is flat. Higher-risk borrowers are capped at $4,000 with only low-risk borrowers petmitted to borrow $25,000+.
  • The most dominant term is the 36-month term length. 12-month terms are rare and limited to smaller amounts while 60-month terms are reserved for larger balances ($10,000).

Borrower APR Across Credit Rating Tiers¶

This plot will display a comparison of BorrowerAPR across all 7 ordered credit tiers in Prosper (AA through HR). These credit tiers represent credit risk, and the higher the rating (HR = High Risk) the higher the risk. We see as credit ratings risk increase the median interest rate climbs up. In the highest risk category, HR, the data is heavily compressed against the platform's interest rate cap of 35%, revealing where the massive spike from slide 1 came from.

Does Borrowing More Money Get You a Lower APR?¶

Larger loans seemingly appear to have lower APRs at first glance. But when we facet Loan Amount vs Borrower APR across each of the 7 Prosper Rating tiers we see that within any credit tier the relationship is pretty much flat i.e taking out a larger loan with the same rating tier does not lower your APR and that credit rating is the gatekeeper. Lower-risk ranks like AA and A are granted low rates and are the only ones permitted to borrow large amounts. High risk borrowers are capped at small loan amounts and are restricted to high APRs.
